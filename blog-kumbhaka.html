Why Kumbhaka (Breath Holding) Is Dangerous For Beginners: The Truth About Nadi Shodhana, Emotions & Unconscious Mind Purification

A yogic perspective on Kumbhaka, Nadi Shodhana, emotional purification and the preparation traditionally associated with breath retention.

KumbhakaNadi ShodhanaYama NiyamaPranayama

But tradition says the exact opposite.

Holding the breath is NOT an achievement. It is a responsibility. And 99% of us are not ready for it.

1. What is Kumbhaka Really? - Not Just Holding Breath

In Yoga Shastra, Kumbhaka is not "holding breath".

Kumbha means pot. Kumbhaka means making your body like a sealed pot where Prana is contained and directed.

There are 2 types:

a) Sahita Kumbhaka: Holding breath with inhalation and exhalation - With support. This is what is taught in Nadi Shodhana today.

b) Kevala Kumbhaka: Breath stops on its own without effort - No inhalation, no exhalation - Ultimate state of meditation.

Logical Point: Kevala happens by itself after years of purification. It cannot be forced. Sahita is the practice for it, but ONLY when your mind vessel is clean.

2. The Logical Example - The Garden of Your Mind

Imagine your mind is a garden.

Your Conscious Mind is the topsoil you can see. Your Unconscious Mind is the deep soil where seeds from your past lives, childhood traumas, karmas, hidden desires, jealousy, anger, lust, even hidden love and attachments are lying as seeds.

Some seeds are positive emotions - "I am a good yoga teacher, I am spiritual, I am loved."

Some seeds are negative emotions - "I am angry, I am jealous, I am not enough, I have fear."

Currently, many seeds are sleeping. They are not active.

What does Kumbhaka do?

When you hold the breath, even with gentle force for one second, you are putting intense pressure on the whole system. Prana has nowhere to go. So it goes inwards and waters ALL the seeds at once - both positive and negative.

Example:

A beginner has hidden anger towards his father from 20 years ago, lying in unconscious mind. He starts practicing Nadi Shodhana with 1:4:2 ratio holding. For 2 months he feels powerful, "advanced". After 3rd month, suddenly he bursts at his wife, his business partner, he gets severe acidity, hormonal imbalance. He thinks yoga harmed him.

No. Yoga did not harm. He watered a sleeping seed at the wrong time with Kumbhaka. He strengthened that anger.

Similarly, you strengthen hidden positive emotion like attachment - "My yoga is best, my guru is best" - This also becomes ego, and later it harms you and others.

That is why for a second also, we should not hold breath without purification.

3. Why Positive is Also Not Positive - The Illusion

We think "strengthening positive emotions is good."

Tradition says: No.

Positive today can be negative tomorrow. Negative today can be positive tomorrow.

Example:

  • Your love for your child is positive. But too much love = attachment = fear of losing = anxiety = hormonal imbalance = controlling behaviour = harm to child.
  • Your anger is negative. But sometimes anger is needed to protect dharma.

Until we become NEUTRAL - Sakshi - Witness - We don't know what is truly positive or negative. We are living in illusion of duality.

Purpose of Yoga is not to strengthen positive. Purpose is to PURIFY both and become neutral.

Kumbhaka before neutrality = You are strengthening the illusion.

4. What Tradition Really Says - Yama & Niyama First

Patanjali Yoga Sutra is very clear in sequence:

Yama -> Niyama -> Asana -> Pranayama -> Pratyahara -> Dharana -> Dhyana -> Samadhi

Today we jump: Asana -> Pranayama with Kumbhaka. We skipped Yama Niyama.

What is Yama Niyama?

  • Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Brahmacharya, Aparigraha
  • Shaucha, Santosha, Tapas, Svadhyaya, Ishwara Pranidhana

This is NOT moral science. This is EMOTIONAL PURIFICATION TECHNOLOGY.

Without practicing Yama Niyama for years, your mind is still dominated by Raga (liking) and Dvesha (disliking). If you do Kumbhaka now, you are locking Raga Dvesha inside with pressure. It creates huge blockages in Nadis, muscles, nervous system.

Modern Science Proves It Too:

  • Breath holding instantly activates sympathetic nervous system - fight or flight. Cortisol spikes. If done without preparation, it creates anxiety, panic, blood pressure fluctuation, emotional instability.
  • Psychology says: Breath retention brings repressed memories and emotions to surface very fast. If you have no tools to process them (which Yama Niyama gives), it leads to emotional flooding.

Even for one second, holding creates blockage in physical and mental body.

5. The Modern Studio Problem - Performance, Certificate, Money

Today's world is fast-track:

  • 200-hour TTC in 21 days - They teach Kumbhaka on Day 3.
  • For attraction: "Learn advanced pranayama, hold breath for 1 minute" - To sell course.
  • For certificate: Students think "I can hold breath, so I am advanced."

Nobody asks: Who is in front of me? What is his unconscious mind carrying? What karmas? What emotional baggage? What is his lifestyle, food, sleep, relationships?

A true Guru will never teach Kumbhaka blindly. He will observe you for years, purify you with Yama Niyama, simple breath awareness without holding, Shatkarmas, mantra, selfless service.

We are all meant to purify ourselves, not to strengthen our inner behaviours.

6. Final Truth: What Should a Beginner Do Instead?

If you are a beginner, your pranayama is this:

1. No Kumbhaka in Asana: Breathe normally. Never hold during asana.

2. No Kumbhaka in Nadi Shodhana: Practice Nadi Shodhana WITHOUT retention. Just Inhale left - Exhale right - Inhale right - Exhale left. Ratio 1:1. Keep it smooth, no force, no sound.

3. Focus on Purification:

  • Practice Yama Niyama in daily life.
  • Practice breath observation - Be aware, observe, check yourself deeply.
  • Practice long, smooth exhalation - Not holding.
  • Make yourself neutral - Not attached to any emotional sentimental life.

When your mind becomes calm, less reactive, when you can observe your emotions without being attached, when your Guru sees you are neutral - THEN he will introduce Sahita Kumbhaka gently.

Don't awaken your past and far future emotions to come early. Don't strengthen them unnecessarily. Let them settle down. Let them get purified.

Conclusion: Be Aware

Just because everyone is teaching holding breath in Nadi Shodhanam, does not mean it is right.

Ask yourself deeply before every practice: Am I purifying or strengthening?
Yoga is not performance. Yoga is purification.

Om Shanti. Observe. Purify. Then Practice Kumbhaka.
